# Unit 5 | Data Protection, Access Control and Organisational Accountability

*Retrospective review of Case Study 11 from the Irish Data Protection Commissioner's 2017 Annual Report.*

The Department of Justice and Equality case illustrates how a seemingly minor administrative error can expose deeper weaknesses in organisational data protection. An employee's medical information remained accessible to approximately 80 staff members for up to three years because a document marked private lacked appropriate access restrictions. The absence of historical access records also prevented the department from establishing the full extent of the disclosure (Data Protection Commissioner, 2017).

The Commissioner found breaches of sections 2A(1) and 2B(1) of the Irish Data Protection Acts 1988 and 2003. Although the incident preceded the GDPR, the underlying concerns remain relevant to Articles 5, 9 and 32, particularly confidentiality, the protection of health information and organisational security.

Attributing the incident to human error explains the immediate mistake but overlooks why the system permitted prolonged exposure without detection. Default access restrictions, periodic permission reviews and retrievable logs could have reduced this risk, although their effectiveness would still depend on consistent enforcement and independent scrutiny. What concerns me most is the gap between having protective measures and being able to establish whether they are working. Without that assurance, organisations may remain unaware of failures until harm has already occurred.
